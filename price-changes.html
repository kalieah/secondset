ECONOMICS • PRICING

Yesterday It Was Cheaper.
What About Now?

Why do prices change when nothing seems to change?

You see something you like. You check the price.

$80.

You come back a few days later.

$95.

Same product. Same store. Same you.

So what happened?

We usually think that prices change because something about the product changed. But most of the time, the story is much bigger than that.

Sometimes, More People Want It

Imagine a concert with 10,000 tickets.

At first, there aren't many people trying to buy them, so the tickets are easier to get.

Then suddenly, everyone wants one.

There are still only 10,000 tickets.

The number of people who want them has changed, but the number of tickets hasn't.

And that's where supply and demand come in.

When more people want something that is limited, its price can go up.

And Sometimes, Nobody Wants It

Now think about a winter jacket at the end of March.

The jacket hasn't changed. It's still the same jacket.

But fewer people want it.

So the store puts it on sale.

Same product. Different demand. Different price.

This happens with much more than clothes. Flights, hotels, concert tickets, food, and even everyday products can change in price depending on what is happening around them.

It's Not Always About the Product

Sometimes, the product stays exactly the same while everything around it changes.

Maybe there isn't enough of it.

Maybe more people suddenly want it.

Maybe it costs more to produce.

Maybe a competitor changed its price.

Maybe it's the right season—or the completely wrong one.

The product didn't necessarily become better or worse.

The situation changed.

So Who Decides the Price?

It's easy to imagine someone sitting behind a desk and simply deciding:

"Let's make this $20 more expensive today."

But prices usually aren't that simple.

Businesses look at demand, supply, competition, costs, timing, and what people are willing to pay.

That's why the price you see today might not be the price you see tomorrow.

And that's what makes prices interesting.

A price isn't just a number.

It can be a signal of what's happening around us.

So next time you see something become more expensive, don't just ask:

“Why did they raise the price?”

Ask:

“What changed?”

Because sometimes, the answer isn't the product.

It's everything around it.
